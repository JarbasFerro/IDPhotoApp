# 01 — Product definition

## 1. Purpose

ID Photo App helps a person create an identity photo that matches the measurable requirements of a selected country and document type, while making the limits of automated validation explicit.

The product should replace the frustrating workflow of searching for dimensions, trying multiple photo editors, guessing at head position/background rules, and arranging copies manually for printing.

## 2. Product promise

**From ordinary portrait to correctly formatted ID photo, with clear guidance and no unnecessary data upload.**

The application must optimize for trust. A technically polished image that violates an official requirement is a product failure. A warning that says “we cannot validate this automatically” is preferable to a false green check.

## 3. Primary jobs to be done

### JTBD-01 — Create a digital ID photo

When I need a passport, visa, ID card, licence, permit, or other document photo, I want the app to format my portrait to the required dimensions and composition so that I can submit the file digitally.

### JTBD-02 — Create printable copies

When I need physical photos, I want the app to place correctly sized copies on common paper/photo formats so that I can print them at home or at a photo kiosk without manually measuring or arranging anything.

### JTBD-03 — Know what is wrong before submission

When my photo is unlikely to meet the selected requirements, I want specific guidance so that I can retake or fix the photo before I submit it.

### JTBD-04 — Preserve privacy

When I use an image of my face or my child's face, I want the workflow to work without an account and without uploading the image unless a clearly explained optional feature genuinely requires it.

## 4. Target users

The product should work for:

- adults creating their own ID/document photos;
- parents creating photos for babies or children;
- immigrants/travellers preparing visa or residence-permit photos;
- users who need a professional headshot/CV format but not an official government document;
- users who need several physical copies from one image;
- occasional users who may not understand photo terminology.

The UI must assume low domain knowledge. Terms such as “crown-to-chin,” “pixel density,” or “biometric” require plain-language explanations.

## 5. Product principles

### P-01 — Trust before conversion

Do not place ads, upsells, or paywalls inside the critical correction path. If monetization exists, the user must still understand what is wrong with the image before being asked to pay.

### P-02 — Official source over crowdsourced rule

Every document profile should identify where its requirements came from and when they were last reviewed. Unsourced specifications cannot be published as “official.”

### P-03 — Deterministic geometry

Final dimensions, crop rectangles, print layouts, margins, and DPI metadata must be generated by deterministic code, not by an opaque generative model.

### P-04 — AI assists; it does not redefine identity

Automation may detect the face, estimate landmarks, segment the subject, identify likely quality problems, and suggest corrections. It must not cosmetically alter facial identity for an official-photo workflow.

### P-05 — Reversible editing

The source image is immutable. Every edit must be reproducible from parameters and removable without quality loss from repeated re-encoding.

### P-06 — Internationalization is structural

Country, language, measurement units, paper size, date format, decimal separator, and supported document types are data/configuration concerns, not hard-coded UI assumptions.

## 6. Core user journey

The intended fast path is:

1. Launch.
2. Choose country.
3. Choose document/use case.
4. Review a short requirement summary.
5. Capture or import photo.
6. Automatic analysis runs locally.
7. User receives one of three states:
   - **Ready** — measurable requirements appear satisfied.
   - **Needs review** — app found warnings or requirements that require human judgement.
   - **Retake recommended** — source quality is likely inadequate.
8. Automatic crop/background/alignment preview is shown.
9. User adjusts if necessary.
10. User selects digital export or print sheet.
11. App exports without watermark unless the monetization model explicitly makes a different promise before the workflow begins.

Target: a valid source photo should require no more than a few primary decisions after document selection.

## 7. MVP scope

### 7.1 Included

- iOS and Android.
- Country + document profile selection.
- Search and favourites/recent document profiles.
- Camera capture.
- Photo-library import.
- EXIF orientation normalization.
- Face detection.
- Basic landmark/head geometry estimation.
- Multiple-face detection and blocking guidance.
- Blur/sharpness screening.
- Exposure screening.
- Basic face-size/head-position checks where measurable.
- Background segmentation/removal where permitted.
- Background replacement with rule-approved colors where applicable.
- Manual crop/position correction.
- Manual background-mask correction or safe fallback when segmentation is uncertain.
- Rule-driven digital export.
- JPEG and PNG where appropriate.
- Print-sheet generator for defined common paper sizes.
- Save/share through platform-native mechanisms.
- Requirement checklist.
- Source/provenance display for document rules.
- Offline core workflow.
- Localization framework.
- Accessibility baseline.
- Crash reporting only if privacy review approves it.

### 7.2 Explicitly deferred

- Printing fulfilment/delivery.
- Account creation and cloud photo library.
- Cross-device sync.
- Automatic clothing replacement.
- Facial retouching/beautification.
- Generative reconstruction of missing face/hair areas.
- OCR of passports or identity documents.
- Automatic government form filling.
- “100% guaranteed accepted” claims.
- Kiosk/business workflow.
- Bulk processing.
- Desktop/web editor.

## 8. Initial document coverage strategy

Do not launch with hundreds of profiles that have not been individually validated. Coverage should expand in controlled waves.

Suggested sequence:

- **Wave A:** a small number of high-demand countries/use cases with clear official specifications.
- **Wave B:** additional passport/visa/ID formats in priority markets.
- **Wave C:** regional and specialized documents.
- **Wave D:** non-official formats such as CV, school, club, or corporate badge photos.

The exact launch-country list is a product decision to be locked after demand research and source verification.

## 9. Quality-state model

Each requirement shown to a user must have one of four machine states:

- `pass` — the app can measure the requirement and the measurement is within tolerance.
- `warn` — the app can measure a risk but confidence/tolerance is not sufficient for a hard failure.
- `fail` — the app can measure the requirement and it is clearly outside the accepted range.
- `manual_check` — the app cannot safely validate the requirement and asks the user to inspect it.

No requirement should silently disappear because the app cannot test it.

## 10. Success metrics

Before release, analytics definitions must be privacy-reviewed. Candidate metrics:

### Product funnel

- document selection → photo selected rate;
- photo selected → valid export rate;
- median time from photo selection to first export;
- percentage of sessions completed without manual adjustment;
- retake rate;
- export error rate.

### Quality

- false-pass rate in controlled validation set;
- false-fail/warning rate;
- crop dimensional error;
- head-position error against ground-truth annotations;
- segmentation defect rate on representative hair/background/skin-tone scenarios;
- print physical-size error after controlled printing tests.

### Reliability

- crash-free sessions;
- image-pipeline failure rate;
- memory-related terminations;
- median/p95 processing latency by device tier.

### User outcome

Where ethically and practically collectable, optional post-use feedback may ask whether the submitted photo was accepted. This must never be represented as authoritative certification data without controlling for self-selection and unknown submission conditions.

## 11. Business-model guardrails

The original product motivation includes frustration with intrusive advertising. Therefore:

- no full-screen interstitial ads in the core photo workflow;
- no ad SDK receives photo content;
- no dark-pattern countdowns;
- no surprise watermark shown only at export;
- pricing, if any, must be shown before a paid-only output is generated;
- a one-time purchase, limited free tier, subscription for advanced/frequent use, or combinations can be evaluated later;
- monetization decisions are deliberately separated from the correctness engine.

## 12. Key product risks

1. **Regulatory drift:** official specifications change.
2. **False assurance:** users may interpret automation as government approval.
3. **Segmentation artifacts:** hair, glasses, veils, skin tones, and complex backgrounds can expose errors.
4. **Camera variability:** devices apply different processing and orientation metadata.
5. **Print-size mismatch:** printers/apps may scale pages unexpectedly.
6. **Children/babies:** pose and expression rules are often different or difficult to assess automatically.
7. **Accessibility:** image-centric editing can exclude screen-reader and low-vision users.
8. **Localization:** government document names and instructions require more than literal translation.
9. **Store/privacy requirements:** permissions and third-party SDK behavior may affect release eligibility.
10. **Over-scoping:** supporting too many countries too early can reduce rule quality.

## 13. Release philosophy

The first release should be narrow and dependable. Add countries and document types only when the rule profile has:

- an official or clearly authoritative source;
- review date;
- validation fixtures;
- output-dimension tests;
- composition tests where rules are measurable;
- user-facing instructions for requirements that remain manual.
