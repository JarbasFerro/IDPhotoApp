# Calipic — Brand foundations

**Status:** Strategic direction approved; visual identity research still open.  
**Scope:** Strategic brand foundation, not final visual-identity specification.

## 1. Purpose

Calipic is a focused iPhone app for creating correctly formatted identity photos and print-ready photo sheets without the complexity, cost, advertising clutter, or guesswork common in the category.

The brand exists to make a constrained photographic task feel unusually simple, beautiful, and complete.

> **Brand purpose:** Make ID photos easy enough for anyone to create and print confidently at home.

The product is not trying to make users feel like photographers, compliance specialists, or image-processing experts. The technology should disappear behind a short, intuitive workflow.

---

## 2. What Calipic should be known for

The two primary brand outcomes are:

1. **The easiest, most intuitive way to make an ID photo on iPhone.**
2. **The easiest way to turn that result into correctly sized physical prints.**

Technical accuracy, privacy, and native iOS quality are essential proof points, but they support the central promise rather than replacing it.

The ideal user summary is:

> “It was simpler than I expected, the result looked beautiful, and printing it was easy.”

---

## 3. Desired emotional outcome

The primary emotional outcome is **satisfaction**.

Calipic should create satisfaction through completion rather than spectacle. The user should feel that an annoying real-world task has been handled cleanly and correctly.

Supporting feelings may include relief, confidence, and control, but they are not the emotional headline.

The design implication is important: Calipic should not chase delight through animation, color, novelty, or personality. The delight comes from the quality of the workflow and the finished result.

---

## 4. Primary audience lens

Calipic remains structurally useful to many audiences, but the brand should be especially understandable and attractive to:

- **people who want to avoid the cost and inconvenience of a photo shop or booth;**
- **parents creating ID photos for children or family members.**

These groups reinforce several design priorities:

- low cognitive load;
- no photography expertise required;
- clear instructions;
- quick recovery from imperfect source photos;
- trustworthy handling of faces and family photos;
- strong print output;
- inexpensive commercial model;
- no intrusive advertising.

The product must still work equally well for travellers, visa/residence applicants, and adults preparing their own photos.

---

## 5. Why someone should choose Calipic over a free competitor

Calipic must earn payment through three visible advantages:

1. **Simpler** — fewer decisions and less clutter.
2. **Easier / more intuitive** — the next step is obvious and the app guides rather than exposes tools.
3. **Beautiful** — exceptional visual craft without turning the workflow into decoration.

These are not marketing adjectives only. They are product acceptance criteria.

If a feature is technically powerful but makes the workflow harder to understand, it is not aligned with the brand.

---

## 6. Positioning

> **For people who need an ID or document photo, Calipic is the iPhone app that makes creating and printing the right photo exceptionally simple. It combines clear guidance, private on-device processing, sourced requirements, and native iOS interaction in a focused experience with no unnecessary complexity.**

### Positioning pillars

#### 6.1 Effortless completion

Calipic is a guided task, not a general photo editor. It should eliminate unnecessary choices and hide technical complexity.

#### 6.2 Print is first-class

Physical printing is not a secondary export utility. It is one of the core reasons Calipic exists. Correct physical dimensions, convenient layouts, and clear printing guidance are part of the product promise.

#### 6.3 Privacy by architecture

The core workflow processes the user's face on-device. Privacy should be communicated prominently as a concrete fact, not through generic security imagery.

#### 6.4 Native iPhone quality

Calipic follows current iOS interaction conventions and Apple platform best practices. Distinctiveness must never come at the expense of familiarity, accessibility, or system coherence.

#### 6.5 Quiet visual craft

The product should feel premium because every detail is considered, not because it uses ornamental luxury cues.

---

## 7. Brand personality

The approved five-trait model is:

### Elegant

Calipic should feel considered, balanced, and visually resolved.

Elegant means:

- strong hierarchy;
- excellent spacing;
- restrained composition;
- few visual devices;
- precise details;
- no ornamental excess.

Elegant does **not** mean luxury-fashion styling.

### Friendly

The task should feel approachable even when the underlying rules are strict.

Friendly means:

- short, helpful instructions;
- human language;
- respectful error handling;
- no intimidation or judgement;
- a visible “we” voice where appropriate.

Friendly does **not** mean playful, cute, or childish.

### Photographic

The photo is the product's visual center.

Photographic means:

- portraits dominate the interface;
- image quality is respected;
- framing and composition can influence the brand language;
- marketing is built around real human photography;
- the interface never looks like administrative software.

### Practical

Every visible element should help the user finish the job.

Practical means:

- obvious next actions;
- descriptive feature names;
- useful defaults;
- minimal setup;
- print output treated as part of completion;
- no decorative interaction tax.

### Sophisticated

Calipic should feel mature and deliberately designed.

Sophisticated means:

- restrained color;
- high-quality typography and spacing;
- controlled motion;
- intelligent behavior without technology theatre;
- details that reward attention without demanding it.

Sophisticated does **not** mean cold, elite, or complicated.

## 8. Personality scales

The approved directional scales use 1 as the first pole and 5 as the second pole.

| Dimension | Score | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Warm → Clinical | 3 | Neutral center; neither cozy nor clinical |
| Friendly → Expert | 2 | Clearly friendly, with expertise expressed through behavior |
| Soft → Precise | 3 | Balanced; exact without feeling engineered |
| Playful → Serious | 4 | Serious task, but not severe |
| Expressive → Restrained | 4 | Strong preference for restraint |
| Consumer → Professional | 1 | Deliberately consumer-first and accessible |
| Familiar → Distinctive | 2 | Familiarity wins; distinctiveness is subtle |
| Approachable → Premium | 3 | Balanced; premium through craft, not exclusivity |

---

## 9. What Calipic must never feel like

The strongest anti-traits are:

- **complicated;**
- **AI-branded;**
- **generic;**
- **childish;**
- **cheap.**

A design should also be rejected if it becomes colorful, crowded, confusing, or visually uninteresting.

This creates an intentional tension: Calipic must be extremely restrained without becoming generic. Ownability therefore needs to come from a small number of strong devices rather than visual density.

---

## 10. Design decision hierarchy

When excellent design goals conflict, use this order:

1. **Native iOS familiarity**
2. **Beauty**
3. **Accessibility**

Correctness, privacy, and rule integrity remain non-negotiable product constraints and are not traded against this hierarchy.

This means Calipic should never invent a novel control merely to make the product look distinctive when the native iOS pattern already solves the interaction well.

---

## 11. Product visual expression

### Approved posture

Outside the user's portrait, Calipic should be **extremely minimal**.

The interface should be equally native to Light and Dark Mode rather than establishing one canonical appearance.

Brand expression should be concentrated in:

- the app icon;
- the Calipic symbol;
- photography and framing;
- a restrained accent color;
- crop/framing geometry;
- subtle calibration/alignment motifs;
- photo-paper proportions;
- optical geometry;
- signature motion at a very small number of moments;
- App Store and marketing composition.

### Approved visual references

Promising visual ideas:

- crop corners;
- framing;
- calibration/alignment marks used sparingly;
- photo-paper ratios;
- optical geometry;
- subtle ruler/measurement ideas where they remain photographic rather than engineering-like.

Rejected for the current identity direction:

- registration-mark aesthetics;
- calipers as a visual motif;
- dense technical measurement graphics;
- fake camera hardware chrome.

---

## 12. Logo and app-icon architecture

### Logo architecture

Calipic should develop a **powerful standalone symbol**, with the wordmark playing a secondary supporting role.

The symbol must work at very small sizes and should be capable of becoming the primary app icon identity.

### App-icon objective

The icon should communicate **“ID photo” immediately** rather than choosing abstraction at the expense of category recognition.

The leading concept to explore is:

> **A human face/portrait silhouette integrated with crop corners.**

This is a direction, not an approved execution. The design must avoid looking like Face ID, facial-recognition surveillance, a contact-card icon, a camera app, or generic passport-photo clip art.

### Name semantics

The `Cali-` connection to **calibration / calibrated precision** is useful and may influence secondary brand storytelling or geometry.

It should not lead to a caliper-tool visual identity.

---

## 13. Technology posture

Technology should disappear behind the result.

Do not make AI, computer vision, segmentation, neural processing, or similar implementation language part of the consumer brand unless the user explicitly asks how something works.

Prefer:

- “We prepared the crop.”
- “The background needs a quick check.”
- “Move a little farther away.”

Avoid:

- “AI-powered biometric engine.”
- “Advanced neural processing.”
- “Generative passport-photo technology.”

Calipic should feel intelligent without talking about intelligence.

---

## 14. Voice

The product voice is **calm and concise**.

Calipic may use **“we”** when it makes the interaction more human and accountable.

Example:

> “We couldn't separate the background cleanly.”

Instructions should normally be direct rather than tentative:

> **Move a little farther away.**

not:

> “You may want to move back a little.”

Official-photo workflows should remain straight. Humor should not be part of the core interaction.

Feature names remain descriptive rather than branded:

- Photo Check
- Background
- Position
- Print Sheet
- Requirements

Avoid names such as `Calipic Check` unless a future concept has an unusually strong reason.

---

## 15. Marketing posture

Marketing may be stronger and more direct than in-product compliance language.

A territory such as:

> **Passport photos made right.**

is appropriate for brand/marketing exploration provided legal and product claims remain accurate.

The app itself must still distinguish between measurable checks and requirements that cannot be guaranteed automatically.

Consumer-facing naming direction:

> **Calipic — ID Photos**

Use an en dash in formal brand typography where supported; App Store metadata should follow platform/search constraints as tested.

---

## 16. Privacy posture

Privacy is a **primary brand promise**, not merely a settings-page detail.

The strongest expression is concrete:

> **Your photo stays on your iPhone for the core workflow.**

Privacy should be visible in onboarding, App Store messaging, and relevant export/help contexts without using fear, shields, locks, or exaggerated security claims.

---

## 17. Commercial model

The intended model is credit-based:

1. the first result produced through the tutorial/onboarding experience is free;
2. the user also receives an initial free credit;
3. subsequent completed outputs consume inexpensive purchased credits;
4. no subscription is required for the intended baseline model.

The pricing experience must remain simple and transparent. Credits should represent understandable completed outcomes rather than exposing technical operations as billable events.

### Advertising

“No ads” is strategically compatible with Calipic and with the credit model, but it should be a **supporting proof point rather than the primary proposition**.

Recommended posture:

> **No ads. No subscription.**

This can strengthen perceived simplicity and respect. The main risk is that users may infer the app is paid; the free first result and clear low-cost credits address that directly.

Final public wording remains subject to pricing implementation and App Store review.

---

## 18. Language and internationalization

Initial language target:

- English
- Spanish
- Portuguese (Brazil)

The launch-market sequence should **not** be chosen by founder geography. It should be researched based on:

- iPhone reach;
- mobile willingness to pay;
- ID/passport-photo demand;
- competitive intensity;
- document-rule feasibility;
- localization cost;
- print behavior;
- App Store discoverability.

Current evidence makes the United States a strong candidate for first-market emphasis, but market priority remains a research decision rather than a locked brand preference.

---

## 19. Color strategy

No brand color is approved.

Color will be selected from evidence and testing rather than a generic “color meaning” chart.

The research must consider:

- empirical brand-perception evidence;
- cross-cultural differences;
- portrait/skin-tone interaction;
- Light and Dark Mode;
- accessibility and increased contrast;
- differentiation from direct competitors;
- small App Store icon performance;
- semantic conflicts with pass/warn/fail colors;
- whether the hue supports elegance, friendliness, photographic focus, practicality, and sophistication.

See `03-color-strategy-research.md`.

---

## 20. Brand success test

A mature Calipic experience should make the following statements true:

- I immediately understand that this app makes ID photos.
- I know what to do without learning the interface.
- The photo is always more important than the UI.
- The app feels unusually well made without looking expensive for the sake of it.
- I can create a digital result or real print without leaving the conceptual workflow.
- I understand that my face is handled privately.
- I never need to know which computer-vision technology is running.
- The interface feels completely at home on iPhone.
- The product remains usable with accessibility settings enabled.
- I finish with a sense of satisfaction rather than relief that I escaped a complicated tool.

---

## 21. Creative references

The founder's strongest brand references are:

- **Apple** — native clarity, system coherence, detail, restraint, accessibility, and confidence in simple interfaces;
- **Teenage Engineering** — strong ownability, exceptional industrial/graphic discipline, reduction to essential controls, and the ability to make functional objects visually memorable.

Calipic must not visually imitate either company. The useful lesson is that simplicity can be highly distinctive when proportion, interaction, typography, iconography, and detail are designed rigorously.
