# Calipic — Brand discovery questionnaire

This document captures the founder/product decisions needed to turn the current product philosophy into a durable Calipic brand system.

Questions are intentionally broader than logo/color selection. The answers will drive positioning, identity, product tone, visual language, App Store presentation, and implementation tokens.

## How to use this document

- Answer instinctively where possible; there is no need to write polished prose.
- `A/B/C` answers are enough when one option is clearly preferred.
- `Unsure` is a valid answer. Uncertain decisions can be explored through concepts/prototypes.
- Existing product principles remain in force unless an answer explicitly proposes changing one.
- Decisions should later be copied into `99-brand-decisions-log.md` once accepted.

## Current inherited hypotheses — not yet brand decisions

Based on the existing product documentation, these are reasonable starting hypotheses. They should be tested against the answers above.

1. Calipic is **quietly premium**, not luxury-coded.
2. The core emotional outcome is **confidence without anxiety**.
3. The product personality is **precise, calm, trustworthy, human, photographic**.
4. The interface should feel **restrained and native**, not highly themed.
5. The user's face/photo should remain the strongest visual element.
6. The brand should make technical intelligence feel mostly invisible.
7. Privacy should be a credible proof point rather than fear-based marketing.
8. The visual system should avoid looking governmental, bureaucratic, or like a generic AI utility.
9. The app icon needs strong recognizability at small sizes and should not rely on a generic camera glyph.
10. Product copy should be plain-language, actionable, calm, and explicit about uncertainty.
11. “Measured automatically” versus “review manually” could become a meaningful trust signature.
12. Brand expression should remain compatible with system typography, semantic materials, SF Symbols, Dynamic Type, and native Liquid Glass.

These hypotheses may be retained, changed, or rejected during discovery.
